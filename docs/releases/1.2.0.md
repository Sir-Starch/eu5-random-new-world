# Random New World 1.2.0 — development candidate

RNW 1.2.0 gives the New World's societies a shared past. The 1337 start follows
three centuries of simulated political change, cooperation, conflict, reform and
migration. You can read that history in the generator and see its surviving
consequences in starting countries, governments and diplomacy. The Old World
stays vanilla.

This candidate targets **EU5 1.4.7 / Steam build 26377361**, with pending algorithm
epoch **61**. It is undergoing final acceptance and has not been published.

## A past that shapes the start

Societies act according to eight independent dimensions, their population,
institutions, neighbours and physical routes. Unions can endure or divide;
agreements can create trust, obligation or dependence. Migration moves existing
population while retaining its culture and faith. Each lasting change records
its causes. Chronicles keep the events that shaped a country, such as unions,
divisions, conquests, submissions and pacts. A quiet past stays short.

The final start reflects those outcomes: ownership and capitals, governments and
positions, population, technology, settlements, roads and relations. Existing
stories and formables use the surviving countries and their actual relationships.
Historical opinion names explain a cause and date. Protection can appear as a
guarantee without making the protected country a subject.

Regions differ. Each large region of the New World starts with its own political
setting: a patchwork of small states, two or three rival powers, or a paramount
realm that prefers clients to conquest. **Extraordinary peoples** seeds rarer
ambitions: a great realm, a sea league, a highland confederation or a sacred
centre. Every ambitious centre is either prudent or overreaching, and must still
win its place through the same simulation. It receives no free territory or
guaranteed success, so a world can end without any great empire. Unfulfilled
ambitions remain visible in the regional overview.

Large realms are possible because administrative capacity grows with
centralization, integration and technology. A realm that holds more than it can
integrate may lose conquered provinces, which become states again. Subjects
submit after a real defeat or under a real threat, and they can regain their
independence when their lord weakens, overreaches or suffers a defeat elsewhere.
Neighbouring societies tend to share institutions, and councils arise from
alliances and unions rather than from ordinary trade.

## Read your world's history

After revealing a world, either generator window shows society profiles,
relationships, chronicles and regional changes. Place links take you to the map.
Relationships distinguish remembered experience from the treaties projected into
EU5. The compact world file carries the recorded history through import and
installed-world recovery.

In EU5, the voluntary **Our History** decision opens the selected country's
chronicle. It has no cost and does not open automatically at campaign start.
Chronicles and historical opinion names support all eleven official languages.
The new game interfaces remain subject to the candidate's final engine check.

## Choose historical turbulence

Choose **Calm**, **Varied** or **Turbulent** on the World tab, or adjust the slider
under the advanced peoples controls. Calm worlds keep older states and more
consolidated realms. Turbulent worlds have more, smaller states, more recent
border changes and more successor states. It does not guarantee a number of wars
or a stronger country for the player. Varied is the default.

## Compatibility

Generate a new world to use the historical start. Existing worlds and saves keep
their own files and algorithm; updating the generator does not rewrite them.
Older world files remain readable and show that no history was recorded.
Continue each campaign with its exact world mod and EU5 version.

The generator hub stays disabled in the playset; enable only the generated world.
Generated maps and game-derived assets are created locally and are not included
in public generator packages. Windows binaries remain unsigned.

An island-world construction fix also ensures a generated region has room for
every original child-area key before names and profiles are assigned.

When a land region reaches only the Old World sea boundary, its boundary water
stays in the protected ocean hierarchy instead of requiring a separate American
coastal sea area. This lets wrapped Wall worlds retain their real ocean routes.
