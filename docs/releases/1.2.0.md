# Random New World 1.2.0

RNW 1.2.0 gives the New World's societies a shared past and coherent starting geopolitics.
The 1337 start follows three centuries of simulated political change, cooperation,
conflict, reform and migration. You can read that history in the generator and see its
surviving consequences in starting countries, governments, diplomacy, laws and formable
ambitions. It also adds **Omerika**, a New World shaped like the Americas, and an option to keep
the game's real Americas and generate only their societies. The Old World stays vanilla.

This release targets **EU5 1.4.7 / Steam build 26377361**, with algorithm epoch **61**.
It incorporates the 1.2.0 history and geopolitics milestones, formable goals and play consequences,
the Workshop feedback items, and fixes from owner play sittings.

## 1. Simulated prehistory (1037–1337)

- **300 years of causal history:** Societies act according to eight independent dimensions,
  population, institutions, neighbours and physical routes. Unions endure or divide; agreements
  create trust, obligation or dependence. Migration moves existing population while retaining
  culture and faith. Chronicles record each lasting change, its causes and its date.
- **Historical turbulence control:** Choose **Calm**, **Varied** (default) or **Turbulent**
  on the World tab, or adjust the slider under Advanced → Peoples. Calm worlds keep older
  states and more consolidated realms. Turbulent worlds have more, smaller states, more
  recent border changes and more successor states.
- **Regional political settings:** Large regions develop distinct political settings: a
  patchwork of small states, two or three rival powers, a paramount realm that prefers clients
  to conquest, or a league/confederation.
- **Extraordinary peoples as seeded ambitions:** Great realms, sea leagues, highland confederations
  and sacred centres start as ambitions that must win their place through the simulation with
  prudent or overreaching strategies. They receive no free territory or guaranteed success, so
  a world can end without any great empire.
- **Conservative demographics:** Population is strictly conserved across all political transitions,
  conquests and migrations. Typed pop cohorts retain their culture, faith and peasant/tribesman
  identity without artificial creation or erasure.

## 2. Starting geopolitics, diplomacy and laws

- **Laws and policies accepted by the engine:** Every starting monarchy, republic and theocracy
  opens with policies that base-game countries of the same government hold, matching its societal
  values. Laws requiring unembraced institutions (such as feudal law or republican foundation
  before legalism) or unheld at tech level 2 (such as royal court customs) are omitted cleanly,
  preventing engine rejection warnings and startup cleanup (`eu5_laws.py`).
- **Treaties within diplomatic capacity:** Starting alliances and guarantees are budgeted so no
  country exceeds what two alliances between equals cost (capped at 6 capacity points). Excess
  historical relationships remain preserved in chronicles and opinions rather than overburdening
  small states with unsustainable treaty maintenance (`history_diplomacy.py`).
- **Historical opinions on the EU5 scale:** Remembered events generate opinions (*Goodwill*,
  *Contention*, and historical grievances) proportioned to EU5's native opinion values and decay
  rates, replacing arbitrary low-impact points.
- **Societal values in the generator window:** The Society tab lists all eleven societal values
  the game displays at the start, in the game's order, showing both poles, leaning and the
  event that shaped each value.
- **Start development matching the game:** Location cards and the Development map lens show
  starting development computed using EU5's exact rules, including river size factors calibrated
  from game saves.
- **Settle the frontier for all New World states:** Rebinds `settle_the_frontier_advance` so that
  every land country with an American capital can settle adjacent unowned frontier land, removing
  vanilla's arbitrary region gate that locked out over half of New World states.

## 3. Storytelling, missions and formables

- **Our History told as a narrative story:** The in-game *Our History* decision and the
  generator's chronicle pane present each country's past as a connected story (origins, pivotal
  trials, broken unions, wars, and its standing in 1337) rather than a list of raw event dates.
- **Formable nations ladder and regional realms:**
  - Level 2 confederations and maritime leagues.
  - Level 3 river unions and cultural commonwealths (up to 60 places).
  - Level 4 **Realm of a region**: one for each generated region with multiple states, asking
    for 40% of the region and its greatest city (throne).
  - Level 5 **Empire of the New World** (fantasy layer): 50% of the continent, empire rank,
    and hegemony.
  - **National formables**: for independent colonial shores or Christian native states, renamed
    in their founders' tradition.
- **Formables as missions (*Road to X*):** Every formable is offered as a mission to hold its
  seat, gather half its required land, and proclaim the nation.
- **Cores on formation:** Proclaiming a formable nation grants cores across its territory,
  opening the game's `cb_conquer_province` against remaining holders.
- **Restorations of divided realms:** Realms that broke apart before 1337 can be restored by
  their living successor states, presenting other heirs with the choice to recognize or contest
  the restored crown.
- **AI expansion goals:** Countries that can form level-3+ nations receive AI expansion scoring
  and target weights toward the required territory.
- **Events of the recorded past:** Countries can remember lost ancestral lands, old pacts,
  divided kin, or sacred centres held under another's peace, choosing whether to press claims
  or renew friendships.

## 4. Geography and seas

- **Substantial enclosed inland seas & small basins as lakes:** Enclosed basins are classified
  before and after strait narrowing. Basins reaching the inland-sea threshold become navigable
  inland seas with ports and coastal areas, but without ocean currents. Small cut-off pockets become
  lakes, preventing generation failures ("generated sea zones cannot reach Old World seas") while
  preserving connected ocean routes.
- **Omerika, a familiar New World reimagined:** A new world type whose land keeps the general
  shape of the Americas (North and South America joined by Central America) while the coasts, bays,
  peninsulas, straits and inland seas are new. The Americas are read from your installed game when
  the world is made and used only as a low-frequency template; nothing of them is shipped. **Likeness
  to the Americas** (Advanced, 50% by default) sets how closely the land follows them; at 100% the
  Americas are plainly recognisable with new coasts and seas. Narrow parts (Central America,
  Yucatan, Baja, Florida, Chile) are held against the noise, and from 30% a natural isthmus joins the
  two great landmasses. Generation fails with a message rather than producing land that falls apart.
- **Keep the real Americas (Omerika, peoples only):** The Americas stay exactly as the game draws
  them and only their societies are generated by the same stages as every world: peoples, states,
  cultures, religions, the 1037–1337 history, laws, development, the AI, stories and formables.
  The mod holds no map file, places keep the game's names, and the world is made in under a minute.
  Land, ocean, map and resource settings do not apply; peoples can be made again. Its behaviour on
  the real map has not been observed in the game.
- **Faster continents:** *Choose a continent* draws its six sketches side by side and keeps them, and
  the area of the game's map that a New World replaces is chosen once per game version. Terrain,
  mountains and vegetation no longer wait for the rest of the generator. Worlds are byte-identical to
  before; the cache of made land stays bounded. A world takes about three and a half minutes on the
  reference machine.
- **Realistic American regional layout:** Subcontinents are organized into north-to-south bands,
  with regions mapped place-first to mirror the Americas (Alaska in the northwest, West Coast in
  the west, Central America in the south).
- **Regions named after local peoples:** Generated regions take names from the primary culture
  of their land, matching the names shown in EU5. Place cards note the vanilla region whose rules
  apply (*Counts as Alaska in the game's rules*).

## 5. Generator workbench and delivery

- **Responsive world switching:** Map and atlas loading run on a cancellable background worker,
  with an LRU cache keeping the four most recently viewed maps in memory. Switching worlds in the
  library is instant and smooth.
- **Stable generation ETA countdown:** The estimation dependency graph separates world generation
  from subsequent vanilla reads, eliminating countdown sawing between values. Late stages hold the
  timer steady while small deviations smoothly adjust the pace.
- **Queue fault tolerance:** If a queued world fails, it is marked with its error and retained
  in the queue while remaining worlds continue generating. Completed worlds appear in the library
  immediately.
- **Version protection for older epochs:** Prevents accidental map corruption when rerolling peoples
  or resources on worlds created by older generator releases. Older worlds are protected with a
  clear explanation and guidance.
- **Playset conflict warnings:** The world page in both windows and the diagnostics report warn if
  another active mod overrides RNW files or if multiple RNW world mods are enabled simultaneously.
- **Terra Incognita in the lobby moved to the World tab:** The toggle to keep the New World masked
  on the country selection map is located on the World tab and is enabled by default.
- **Reveal the New World at start (multiplayer):** Optional world setting enabling human players
  to discover the New World upon campaign start after the lobby, while computer countries explore
  normally.
- **Total Chaos naming & settings redraw:** Untouched random draws preserve the "Total Chaos" name
  in codes and library records; manual adjustments switch to "Custom world". Added "Randomize settings
  again" button in both windows.
- **Native Linux window startup:** Packaged Tauri window uses the system library search path,
  allowing WebKitGTK and ICU to load their matching system runtime on Arch Linux and other rolling
  distributions.
- **Full 11-language localization:** All new texts, chronicles, formables, laws, missions, and
  interface labels are fully translated across all 11 official EU5 languages.

## 6. Compatibility and saves

Generate a new world to use the 1.2.0 history and geopolitics start. Existing worlds and saves
retain their own files and algorithm epoch; updating the generator does not rewrite them.
Older world files remain readable and indicate that no history was recorded. Continue each
campaign with its exact world mod and EU5 version.

A world code made by 1.1.0 (algorithm epoch 60) can give another world in 1.2.0 (epoch 61), and
the generator refuses new peoples, new resources and *Same land, change settings* on a world made
by an earlier epoch, with the reason shown. Use **New world with these settings** to get a new land.

Enable **one generated RNW world** at a time; the Workshop item supplies the generator, not a
world. Restart EU5 before switching maps. Allow about **7.5 GB of free RAM** for generation. Windows
binaries remain unsigned, and Smart App Control may block them. The new window needs WebView2 on
Windows or WebKitGTK 4.1 on Linux; the classic window is the fallback.
