# Legal and distribution notes

> Historical snapshot retained from before the 2026-10-29 documentation cleanup.
> Dated plans, checklists and uncertainties below describe their original context;
> current instructions are in [AGENTS.md](../../AGENTS.md) and the [documentation index](../README.md).

This is project risk analysis, not legal advice. [SOURCE] The [Paradox User Agreement](https://legal.paradoxplaza.com/eula), accessed 2026-10-13 and updated 2026-01-21, grants creation and public sharing of noncommercial UGC/mods under §5, subject to lawful material and rights; the creator's rights cover original additions, not Paradox or third-party game content. §1 reserves rights in the game, and §8 covers launcher/update behavior. The agreement says special game-specific rights may vary, so these general terms are not a blanket license to redistribute EU5 assets (C15).

**Interoperability research (2026-10-22).** Reverse-engineering of closed formats and binary files of EU5 is recorded case by case, with the necessity, the lack of official means, the data touched and how compatibility is kept without distributing Paradox's resources, in the [interoperability register](../research/interoperability.md) (standing rule of the owner, with Directive 2009/24/EC as the EU frame). The first entry is the spline network, whose generated file holds derived vanilla strips and so stays in the unshared built world like `locations.png`.

**Generated mods.** A package containing only original scripts and art may be shareable under the UGC rules [INFERRED C15], subject to EU5-specific terms and other contributors' rights. A full modified `locations.png` derived from the game's raster may carry Paradox-owned material even if many pixels change [INFERRED C03,C15]; distribution permission is unresolved [UNKNOWN]. The conservative design is to distribute the generator and original templates, then generate any derived map locally from the player's lawful installation without uploading that output. A user may still create a local mod for their own play; public redistribution should be reviewed separately. The D0 `rnw generate` outputs (`locations.png`, `rivers.png`, preview) are such derived files: they stay in the gitignored `generated/` folder and are not committed or distributed [INFERRED C15,C28].

**Generator delivery.** Decided on 2026-10-21 ([ADR 0009](../adr/0009-hub-mod-with-generator.md), replacing ADR 0004 and ADR 0008): one free Workshop item carries the data mod and the generator; the generator builds the world on the player's machine from the player's installation, and the world, which holds derived vanilla files, is never uploaded. No Workshop subscription, upload or publish action has been performed.

These are the current source terms we found, not a conclusion that every proposed file is cleared for upload. [UNKNOWN C267] The review records interpretive limits for platform terms and game-derived material. The owner confirms the rules review is complete for the audited generator hub (2026-10-29, C437); this note does not require another review before its publication. The conservative rule remains: publish the generator and original assets only; generate derived worlds locally and never upload them.

**Licence (decided 2026-10-21 at the owner's request).** The project's code and mod files are under the **Mozilla Public License 2.0** (`LICENSE`, Mozilla's text; `license = "MPL-2.0"` in `pyproject.toml` and `native/Cargo.toml`). Original art and prose added later, such as Workshop images, go under CC BY 4.0. A first choice of GPL-3.0-only was replaced the same day after a second opinion the owner relayed, because the owner's interests include the chance that Paradox might one day take the mechanism into the game. Reasons:

- *Our files stay open.* Whoever distributes our files, changed or not, must offer their source under the MPL and keep the notices (§3.1–3.2); a re-upload with changes to our generator cannot close those changes. A permissive licence (MIT, Apache-2.0) would allow that.
- *Room for a larger, closed work.* The MPL lets our files be combined into a closed program as long as our files stay open (§3.3, "Larger Work"). A studio's engine or a closed tool can use them without a separate licence; GPL-3.0 would forbid that and would mostly bind other modders, while a licence the owner grants Paradox directly, or Paradox's UGC grant below, would cover Paradox in any case.
- *Signing.* The SignPath Foundation signs only projects under an OSI-approved licence without commercial dual licensing (ADR 0009, Trust on Windows); the MPL-2.0 is one.
- *Compatible with every dependency* (C110): Python (PSF-2.0), PySide6 (LGPL-3.0), NumPy (BSD-3-Clause with 0BSD, MIT, Zlib, CC0 parts), Pillow (MIT-CMU), Typer and Rich (MIT), Pygments (BSD-2-Clause), the Rust crates (MIT or Apache-2.0, BSD-2-Clause, Unicode-3.0). The files carry no "Incompatible With Secondary Licenses" notice, so GPL projects may use them too (§3.3).
- *Not non-commercial.* Non-commercial licences are not open source (no SignPath), and Paradox's terms already require mods to be free and non-commercial.

Limits: copyright protects the code, not the idea of a generated New World; anyone, Paradox included, may build the same idea anew. The licence binds others only as far as the owner holds copyright; how far code written with AI assistance is protected depends on the human contribution and the jurisdiction [UNKNOWN]. Paradox's User Agreement (§5) has the creator grant Paradox a broad, sublicensable licence to published UGC, independent of the MPL, for the owner's own work. The EU5 Steam listing links to that agreement, but the exact scope for an item uploaded through Valve's Workshop and its interaction with Valve's Workshop terms remain [UNKNOWN C267]. That grant cannot extend to the third-party libraries in the item, which keep their own licences (their texts and a notices file travel in the item). The generated worlds are the program's output, not covered by our licence, and carry Paradox-derived content for local use only. Outside contributions come under the MPL with a sign-off (the Developer Certificate of Origin); the MPL already lets them into a larger closed work, so only a change to a different licence would need a contributor licence agreement. The mod's script files (interface, localization, game rules) are our own text in Paradox's formats; anything copied from vanilla stays Paradox's and is not relicensed.

**Public repository (2026-10-24).** Before the repository was first published on GitHub (`Sir-Starch/eu5-random-new-world`), every revision of its history was checked for game files and personal data. No game asset, derived raster, log, save or screenshot had ever been committed. Local machine details (installation paths, hardware, operating system) were replaced in all revisions, and the commits carry the maintainer's public address. The rule is kept by tests: `packaging/allowlist.py` rejects any path of the game or of a generated world, both in the repository and in every release archive. The README states that the project is not affiliated with Paradox Interactive and that *Europa Universalis* is Paradox's trademark.

**Third-party references.** [SOURCE] [EU5 Engine Atlas](https://github.com/mattebin/eu5-engine-atlas) advertises MIT but targets 1.3.11; [EU5 Modding Project](https://github.com/HLJSXK/eu5-modding-project) lacks a clear software license in its README; the [Co-op toolkit](https://github.com/Europa-Universalis-5-Modding-Co-op/community-mod-toolkit/) was not reviewed. No third-party code or game assets are vendored. Before using any outside code or data, pin a revision, inspect its license and check redistribution compatibility. The current repository contains only project-authored research, code and synthetic tests [V-EXEC].

**Programs in mods (2026-10-21).** [SOURCE] The [Paradox User Agreement](https://legal.paradoxplaza.com/eula), last updated 2026-01-21, lists "scripts and programs" among UGC (§5), which may be created and made publicly available for free and non-commercially; §7 and the [Rules of Conduct](https://legal.paradoxplaza.com/rules-of-conduct?locale=en) (updated 2022-05-02) forbid cheats, hacks, malicious programs and "hacked data files". Neither names DLLs, code mods or reverse engineering. A generator that reads the player's own installation and writes a data mod, carried in a free Workshop item (ADR 0009), fits that wording; an engine-hooking DLL would be a hack (ADR 0009, alternative rejected) [INFERRED]. The Steam and Paradox distribution terms are summarized above; see [C267](../research/claims-ledger.md) for scope and remaining uncertainty.

**Application dependencies (2026-10-21).** The window uses PySide6 (Qt for Python) under LGPL-3.0, which allows a package that ships the Qt libraries as separate, replaceable files with the licence text and a link to the Qt sources; the Windows layout of ADR 0009 keeps them as they come from the wheel. PyInstaller, kept for development builds on Linux, is GPL-2.0 with an exception for the programs it makes. A third-party notices file belongs to the release (P1).
