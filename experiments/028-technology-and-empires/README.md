# 028 — R7b and R7c: technology, regional families and great empires in game

Date prepared: 2026-10-22. **NOT-RUN: PREPARED, INSTALLED, NOT LAUNCHED.**

- **Hypothesis:** EU5 1.4.3 honours a written `starting_technology_level`, a `country_rank`, generated `road_network` pairs and `opinion` records for new tags and locations. A state's advances follow its level and its regional family. The great empire shows its causes (the Old World's technology, a higher rank, royal roads) and its weaknesses (feared by its independent neighbours, resented by its vassals) in the ordinary windows, and its vassals are valid at the start (the log pattern of C99).
- **Method**, on the installed world below, with EU5 started by the owner:
  1. In the country window of a level-2 state, a level-0 or level-1 state (the report's `technology.tags_at_level` lists the tags) and an empire, check that the researched advances match the levels.
  2. In the advances of one state of each family, check the regional tree: `system_of_tributaries` for Mesoamerica, `valley_irrigation` for South America, `three_sisters` and the northern advances for North America.
  3. For an empire, check its rank title, its roads on the map from the capital to its towns and vassals, the fear of its neighbours and the resentment of its vassals in the diplomacy view, and whether its vassals are valid (the C99 log pattern).
  4. Look for `error.log` lines that name `starting_technology_level`, `country_rank`, `road_network`, `opinion` or `rnw_`. Compare with the baseline using `rnw logdiff`.
  5. Observe 1337–1400 at speed. Does the empire hold, grow or break? Do the colonisers meet it, and does the log stay clean? This is the R7 gate.
